Billing, Support, Well-Architected and Week 2 Review

By the end of today you can Control and forecast your AWS spend, choose the right support plan, describe the six Well-Architected pillars, score yourself on a 20-question exam-style quiz, and ship a mini-project that proves Week 2 skills.

1. Money: how AWS billing actually works

Three pricing principles: pay as you go, save when you commit (Day 7), and pay less as you use more (tiered pricing, e.g. S3 gets cheaper per GB at volume). Data into AWS is generally free; data out to the internet and between Regions is charged.

ToolUse it to
Pricing CalculatorEstimate cost before building (you did this on Day 1)
Billing & Cost Management consoleSee this month's bill
Cost ExplorerGraph past spend, filter by service/tag, forecast
BudgetsAlert (or act) when actual/forecast cost crosses a threshold
Cost and Usage Report (CUR)The most detailed billing data, delivered to S3
Cost allocation tagsLabel resources (project=blog) to see cost per project
Consolidated billing (Organizations)One bill; combined volume discounts
Analogy: phone planBudgets = text message "you've used 80% of your data". Cost Explorer = the usage graph in your phone app. Tags = labelling each family member's line so you know who used what. Calculator = comparing plans before signing up.

3. Design: the Well-Architected Framework (six pillars)

  • Operational Excellence: automate, learn from failures. Security: least privilege, encrypt, trace. Reliability: recover from failure, scale horizontally. Performance Efficiency: right tools, experiment. Cost Optimization: pay for what you need. Sustainability: minimise environmental impact.

Also know the Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF) (people/process/platform perspectives for moving to cloud) and the 6 Rs of migration: Rehost, Replatform, Repurchase, Refactor, Retire, Retain.

4. Mini-project (60–90 min): "Secure personal site"

Combine Week 1 + 2: host your portfolio page (copy of your repo's index.html) on a private S3 bucket behind CloudFront (HTTPS), encrypt the bucket with SSE-KMS or SSE-S3, make sure CloudTrail records it, and add a Budget alarm of US$5. Document it as aws-course/labs/week2-secure-site.md with: an architecture diagram you draw (draw.io or hand-drawn photo), the steps, what broke, and the screenshot of the working HTTPS site. This file is a portfolio piece.

  1. Repeat the Day 11 setup. Add a custom error page (error.html) and set the CloudFront error response to show it for 403/404.
  2. Turn on CloudFront standard logging or note that you've seen the metrics in the distribution's Monitoring tab.
  3. Budgets → Create → Cost budget → Monthly US$5 → alert at 80% actual → your email.
  4. Write the doc, commit, and then tear everything down (keep the doc and screenshot).
